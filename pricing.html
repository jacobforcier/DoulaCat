Why one flat fee

Birth doesn’t run on a clock, so I don’t bill by the hour. A twelve-hour labor and a thirty-six-hour labor cost the same, and you should never be doing arithmetic in the middle of it.

What’s not included

I’m not a medical provider. Doula support doesn’t replace your midwife, OB, or pediatrician, and I don’t perform clinical tasks or make medical decisions.

Paying for care

How families cover the cost

Doula care is more payable than most people assume. A few routes worth knowing about.

HSA and FSA funds

Many families pay for doula support with a health savings or flexible spending account. Some plans ask for a letter of medical necessity from your provider first. I can give you a receipt or superbill for reimbursement.

New Hampshire Medicaid

New Hampshire has passed legislation to cover doula care through Medicaid and created a state doula certification process, but the benefit isn’t active yet. If that changes it will be worth asking about — it’s worth checking the current status when you’re planning.

Private insurance

Most private plans in New Hampshire don’t currently cover doula support. It costs nothing to ask yours, and a receipt from me is what they’ll want if they do.

Verify before launch, and revisit yearly The New Hampshire Medicaid position above reflects public reporting as of 2026: coverage was authorized in the 2023 state budget, SB 337 (July 2024) set up a voluntary state doula certification, and implementation was still in progress. Confirm current status with NH DHHS before publishing. Separately — it is worth Catherine registering for state certification as soon as it opens, so she can bill Medicaid from day one rather than months after.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask

If yours isn’t here, ask me on the consultation — that’s what it’s for.

What does a doula actually do?

Emotional, physical, and informational support before, during, and just after birth. In practice that means helping you prepare and think through your options in pregnancy, staying with you continuously through labor with hands-on comfort techniques, making sure you understand what’s being offered in time to decide, and helping you settle afterward.

Isn’t that what my partner is for?

Your partner is irreplaceable and I’m not there to replace them. But most partners have never done this before, and it’s hard to be both emotionally present for someone you love and calmly on top of positions, timing, and what the monitor means.

My job is to take the second part so they can do the first. Two of the reviews on this site were written by first-time fathers, which tells you how that tends to work out.

Do I need a doula if I’m planning an epidural?

Yes, if you want one. An epidural changes what support looks like — more positioning help, more work keeping labor progressing, more attention to comfort and to you as a person rather than a monitored patient — but it doesn’t remove the need for someone who stays. I have no agenda about how you manage pain. I’ve supported plenty of medicated hospital births.

What if I end up needing a cesarean?

I spent years as an operating room nurse before becoming a doula, so I know exactly what a cesarean involves. If your birth goes that way, I can tell you what’s happening and why in language that actually helps, and support you and your partner through it rather than going quiet because the plan changed.

What’s your philosophy of support?

The birth is yours and so are the decisions. I don’t have a preferred way for you to give birth, and I’m not going to be disappointed in you for changing your mind at hour nineteen.

What I will do is make sure you know what’s being offered, what the alternatives are, and that you have time to ask questions before answering. I don’t speak to your care team on your behalf — I help you speak for yourself, and I back you up once you’ve decided.

What comfort techniques do you use?

Counterpressure, hands-on positioning, movement, breathing, and Spinning Babies techniques for helping a baby find a better position. I’ve had five babies of my own with back labor, so if that’s where you end up, I know from both sides what helps.

Are you certified?

Yes — I’m a Certified Birth Doula through DONA International, CD(DONA), certified in 2025 and current through 2028. I also hold a Bachelor of Science in Nursing (2014), an NRP certificate from the American Academy of Pediatrics, and Spinning Babies workshop training.

Where do you travel?

Manchester and the surrounding southern New Hampshire towns — Bedford, Goffstown, Hooksett, Concord, Nashua and nearby. If you’re a little outside that, ask anyway.

How much does it cost?

Birth doula support is $1,200 as a single flat fee, covering everything from our prenatal visits through your postpartum follow-up. Birth doesn’t run on a clock, so I don’t bill by the hour. Full details are on the pricing page.

Can I use HSA or FSA funds?

Often, yes. Many families pay for doula support this way; some plans want a letter of medical necessity from your provider first. I can provide a receipt or superbill for reimbursement. See paying for care for more.

When should I reach out?

Whenever you start thinking about it. Many families get in touch in the second trimester, but it’s always worth asking even if you’re further along — if I’m free for your due date, I’m free.

What happens if you’re not available when I go into labor?

Every doula needs a plan for this — another birth, illness, or an emergency. Ask me about my backup arrangement on our consultation and I’ll walk you through exactly how it works.

Three answers still needed

1. Backup doula. The placeholder answer above is honest but weak. Nearly every serious client asks this, and very few doula sites answer it up front — naming the arrangement here would be a real advantage.

2. On-call window. When does she go on call — 36 weeks? 38?

3. Client load. How many births does she take per month? Families ask because they want to know they won’t be one of three due the same week.

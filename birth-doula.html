What it actually looks like to have me with you

Not a checklist of services so much as a relationship that starts in pregnancy and doesn’t end when the baby arrives.

Before your birth

We meet to talk properly — about what you’re hoping for, what you’re worried about, what your provider has told you, and what your partner wants their role to be. We go through comfort techniques so they’re familiar before you need them.

If you’re writing a birth plan, I’ll help you word it so it does what you want it to do. That sounds small. It isn’t — how a preference is phrased changes how it’s received, and a plan that reads as a set of clear requests lands very differently from one that reads as a wish list.

And if something about your care isn’t sitting right, we can talk about that too. I’ve helped families find a different provider or a different birth setting when the one they had wasn’t going to give them the birth they wanted.

Between visits

You can call or text me. Questions at 11pm about whether something is normal, last-minute worries the day before an appointment, a link you want a second opinion on — that’s all included, and it’s a real part of the job rather than an imposition on it.

On call

As you approach your due date my phone stays on. When labor starts you call me, and I come when you want me there — not on a schedule, not when a policy says so.

During labor and birth

I stay. That’s the heart of it. Counterpressure and positioning, Spinning Babies techniques, ideas when something stops working, and a steady voice through transition. I’ve had five babies with back labor myself, so if that’s where you end up, you’ll be in very experienced hands.

I’ll also make sure you understand what’s being offered and why, with time to ask questions before you answer. I don’t make decisions for you and I don’t speak to your team on your behalf — I make sure you have what you need to speak for yourself, and I back you up once you’ve decided.

Your partner isn’t sidelined by any of this. Most partners want badly to help and aren’t sure how; part of what I do is show them, so they can be present with you rather than anxious beside you.

After the baby arrives

I don’t leave the moment the baby is out. I stay through those first hours, help with that first feed, and make sure you’re settled before I go home.

Then I follow up. Some families want a visit; some want a video call to talk through what happened, especially if the birth went somewhere they didn’t expect. Processing a birth out loud with someone who was actually in the room matters more than people expect it to. And you can keep asking me questions after — people do, and I answer them.

Specifics to pin down The shape above is accurate, but these numbers should be exact before it goes live: how many prenatal visits are included; when the on-call window opens (36 weeks? 38 weeks?); whether the postpartum follow-up is a visit, a call, or the client’s choice; and the backup-doula arrangement if she’s at another birth or unwell. Nearly every client asks about backup, and very few doula sites answer it up front.

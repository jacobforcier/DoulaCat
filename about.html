About

Hi, I’m Catherine

I fell in love with the world of birth while learning about motherhood myself, over the last decade and five babies of my own.

Before birth work, I was an operating room nurse. I earned my Bachelor of Science in Nursing in 2014 and spent years in the OR, which turns out to be unusual training for a doula and a genuinely useful one. Hospitals don’t intimidate me. I know the vocabulary, I know how the room works, and I know exactly what a cesarean involves — so if your birth takes a turn you weren’t planning on, I can tell you what’s happening in words that actually help.

The rest of what I bring isn’t clinical at all. I’ve had five babies, all unmedicated, all with back labor, in birth centers and at home. I know what transition feels like at three in the morning. I know the specific desperation of back labor, and I know what helps. And I know that what most people need in that moment isn’t advice — it’s someone steady who isn’t going anywhere.

I love educating and assisting parents to have the birth of their dreams. Not the birth I’d choose, or the one a book recommends — the one they want, with enough information to make their own decisions along the way.

Experience

What I’ve been present for

Since December 2022 I’ve supported families through medicated hospital births and unmedicated births in birth centers, and I’ve assisted midwives at home births. I’ve been in the room for straightforward births and for births that surprised everyone in it — including a breech baby born vaginally after three hours of pushing.

I’m comfortable in all three settings, and I have no agenda about which one you choose. Some of the families I’ve worked with wanted an epidural from the outset. Some wanted a birth center an hour from home. Both are good births if they’re the birth you wanted.

A note on what a doula is and isn’t

I provide emotional, physical, and informational support. I am not your medical provider: I don’t perform clinical tasks, make medical decisions, or deliver babies, and I don’t speak to your care team on your behalf. What I do is make sure you understand what’s being offered and have room to decide — and then support whatever you decide.
